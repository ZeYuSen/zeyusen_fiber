# Reinforcement Sizing and Resin Compatibility: A Buyer's Guide

A reinforcement and a resin only perform as a laminate when their surface chemistry matches. Glass fibers carry a **sizing** applied during manufacture, and mats carry a **binder** that holds the strands together. Both are engineered for specific resin families. Ordering the right weight and weave but the wrong sizing can leave a laminate that will not wet out or bond properly.

This guide explains what sizing and binder do, where epoxy and polyester or vinyl ester systems differ, and which fields to confirm before a purchase. It is a buyer-orientation guide, not a substitute for the supplier's technical data sheet.

## Sizing and Binder Are Not the Same Thing

- **Sizing (or size / finish):** a thin coating applied to glass filaments during forming. It protects fibers during handling and, critically, provides a chemical bridge (often a silane coupling agent) between the glass and a particular resin family.
- **Binder:** an additional agent used mainly in chopped strand mat and some non-woven products to hold strands in place until the resin wets them out. In many mats the binder is designed to soften or dissolve once the correct resin is applied.

Both are chosen for a target resin. A fabric or mat described only by weight and weave is not fully specified until the sizing and binder basis is known.

## The Chopped Strand Mat and Epoxy Example

The clearest compatibility trap is chopped strand mat (CSM). Much CSM is held together with a **styrene-soluble (emulsion) binder** that is designed to dissolve in the styrene monomer present in polyester and vinyl ester resins. When the binder dissolves, the strands relax and the mat wets out and conforms.

Epoxy contains no styrene monomer, so it does not dissolve that binder the same way. As Fiberglass Warehouse states, fiberglass mat is "commonly bonded with a styrene-based binder, designed to dissolve in polyester and vinyl ester resins," and because "epoxy lacks this component," it "cannot fully saturate or adhere to the fibers within the mat" in the same manner.

The practical consequences for a buyer:

- **Emulsion/styrene-soluble CSM** is aimed at polyester and vinyl ester layups.
- If a project uses **epoxy**, confirm a mat with a binder qualified for epoxy (some are marketed specifically for it) rather than assuming any CSM will work.
- Do not treat "chopped strand mat" as a single interchangeable product. The binder chemistry is part of the specification.

This is a compatibility rule about binder and resin chemistry. It does not mean epoxy is inferior; it means the reinforcement must be matched to the resin actually being used.

## Match Sizing to the Resin Family

Glass fabric sizings are also formulated for resin families. A silane coupling agent tuned for epoxy does not necessarily perform the same in polyester, and vice versa. Suppliers commonly describe a sizing as suitable for one or more of epoxy, vinyl ester, polyester, or phenolic. Confirm:

- Which resin family the sizing is qualified for.
- Whether a "multi-compatible" size is genuinely validated for your resin, or only tolerated.
- Any shelf-life or storage condition that affects sizing performance.

## Resin Families at a Glance

This is orientation only; use supplier data and your own qualification for any real decision.

| Resin family | Typical draw for buyers | Reinforcement note |
|---|---|---|
| Polyester | Lower cost, styrene-based, common in general FRP | Works with styrene-soluble CSM binders |
| Vinyl ester | Better chemical/water resistance than many polyesters, styrene-based | Also dissolves styrene-soluble binders |
| Epoxy | High adhesion and mechanicals, no styrene monomer | Needs sizing/binder qualified for epoxy |
| Phenolic | Fire/smoke performance focus | Confirm compatible sizing explicitly |

The point is not to rank resins. It is that the reinforcement's sizing and binder must be chosen for whichever resin the part actually uses.

## What to Confirm Before Ordering

Build these into the RFQ so compatibility is not left to assumption:

1. **Intended resin system** stated up front (family and, if known, product).
2. **Sizing / finish** and the resin families it is qualified for.
3. **Binder type** for any mat (styrene-soluble/emulsion, powder, or epoxy-compatible).
4. **Process** (hand lay-up, infusion, RTM) — some binders behave differently under resin flow.
5. **Data sheet and any compatibility statement** in writing.
6. **A representative wet-out/cure trial** on your resin before committing to volume.

## Failure Modes to Watch

- **Poor wet-out:** resin sits on the surface or leaves dry patches — often a sizing/binder mismatch.
- **Weak interlaminar bond:** the fiber–matrix interface never fully forms.
- **Mat that will not conform:** binder not dissolving as expected with the chosen resin.
- **Inconsistent lots:** undocumented sizing changes between batches.

Any of these can appear even when weight and weave are exactly as ordered, which is why sizing and binder belong in the specification.

## FAQ

### Can I use chopped strand mat with epoxy resin?

Only if the mat's binder is compatible with epoxy. Much chopped strand mat uses a styrene-soluble (emulsion) binder made to dissolve in the styrene monomer in polyester and vinyl ester resins. Epoxy has no styrene monomer, so it does not dissolve that binder the same way and may not wet out or bond the mat properly. If your project uses epoxy, confirm a mat with an epoxy-qualified binder rather than assuming any CSM will work.

### What is the difference between sizing and binder?

Sizing is a thin coating applied to glass filaments during manufacture that protects the fiber and provides a chemical bridge to a specific resin family. Binder is an additional agent, used mainly in mats, that holds the chopped strands together until resin wets them out. Sizing is about the fiber–resin interface; binder is about mat integrity and wet-out behavior. Both are formulated for target resins and both belong in a specification.

### Does the resin or the reinforcement determine compatibility?

Both. The reinforcement's sizing and binder are engineered for particular resin families, so compatibility is a match between the two, not a property of either alone. State the intended resin system in the RFQ, then confirm the sizing and binder are qualified for it, and validate with a wet-out and cure trial before committing to volume.
